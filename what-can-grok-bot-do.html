What can Grok Bot do?

What Grok Bot can actually do for you depends on your plan and your settings, not just the product itself. This page covers general categories of use, not an exhaustive feature list, and it is independent commentary, not an official spec sheet.

Run more than one focused bot

Grok Bot supports running multiple bots, each scoped to a different job, rather than one bot handling everything. A workable starting layout, based on the guide's own-use pattern, is one generalist bot for everyday questions plus a couple of specialists for recurring jobs. You do not need a dozen bots for this to be useful.

The useful part is not the number of bots, it is the handoffs between them: giving one bot a narrow brief, having it hand a result to another bot, and keeping each one's context from bleeding into jobs it was not scoped for. The guide's chapter on using more than one bot includes a decision diagram for "should this be its own bot?" that walks through exactly this.

Grok Bot prompts: example categories

The guide's 15 tested prompts fall into four categories: travel, deals, errands, and monitoring or inbox tasks. Below are generic, illustrative examples of the shape those prompts take, not the guide's actual tested prompts and not a guarantee of any specific result. 

Example (illustrative, travel-style):
"Compare flight options for [dates] between [city A] and [city B].
List price, total travel time, and number of stops. Flag anything
with a layover over three hours."
Example (illustrative, monitoring-style):
"Check [a specific page or listing] once a day and tell me only
if the price drops below [amount]. Otherwise stay quiet."

Both examples are generic prompt shapes meant to show how a scoped request looks, written for this page rather than pulled from the guide. What actually works for you depends on your plan, your settings, and the specifics of your request.

Usage limits depend on your plan

Grok Bot is only available on Premium, Super, or Ultra, an eligible legacy or linked plan, or a team seat; Free and Plus do not include it at all. Once you have access, usage draws from a shared weekly pool rather than a per-bot allowance, and top-ups are available if you run through that pool. See Grok Bot pricing explained for the full breakdown and current numbers.

Grok Bot privacy depends on your settings

Account-level privacy settings affect how Grok Bot behaves, not just whether you can use it. Accounts on Privacy Mode (Legacy) are asked to switch to Privacy Mode before they can start using Grok Bot at all, which is a sign that privacy configuration is part of setup, not an afterthought. For anything beyond that basic account setting, check your current privacy controls directly and treat the guide's privacy chapter and official documentation as the places to look, rather than assuming defaults.

A few practical Grok Bot tips

  • Give each bot a specific name and a one-line job description, not a generic label.
  • Do a written handoff between bots instead of letting one thread sprawl to cover everything.
  • Check Settings for Update versus Reset before assuming a stuck bot needs to be rebuilt from scratch.
  • Confirm your plan actually includes Grok Bot before troubleshooting why it will not respond.
