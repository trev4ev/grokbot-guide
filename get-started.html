How to get started with Grok Bot

A short, free walkthrough for anyone opening Grok Bot for the first time. If you want the full version, with 15 tested prompts and a pricing table, see the guide.

Step 1: check you have an eligible plan

Before anything else, confirm Grok Bot is actually part of your plan. It is not included on every plan. As of October 31, 2026, Grok Bot requires Premium, Super, or Ultra on The X Subscription, either an individual or a team seat, or an eligible legacy or linked plan. The Free and Plus plans do not include it, so if you are on one of those, upgrading is the first step, not a settings change.

The official pricing page suggests Premium for people new to AI tools in general, which lines up with what most beginners need: enough usage to actually use Grok Bot day to day without immediately hitting a limit. Prices change, so check the current numbers on x.ai/pricing before you upgrade. Our pricing explained page breaks the plans down in more detail.

Step 2: handle Privacy Mode (Legacy)

If your account is on Privacy Mode (Legacy), you'll be asked to switch to Privacy Mode before you can start using Grok Bot. This is a one-time account setting, not something you redo for every bot. Do it before you try to create your first bot, so the setup flow does not stall halfway through.

Step 3: open Grok Bot and start your first bot

There is no separate Grok Bot login. If your account already has an eligible plan and Privacy Mode is sorted out, Grok Bot is available from inside the product you already use. The official product page at x.ai/bot has the current description and entry point if you are not sure where to look.

When you create your first bot, give it a specific name and a one-line job description instead of a generic label. A bot named "Research" that is actually scoped to "find and compare flight options" behaves more predictably than a bot named "Assistant" that is asked to do everything. 

Example (illustrative, not from the guide):
"You are my travel research bot. Your only job is finding flight
options and summarizing prices, times, and layovers. Do not book
anything. Ask me before assuming a date range."

That is a generic example to show the shape of a scoped job, not a claim about what any specific prompt will do for you; results depend on your plan and settings.

Step 4: get comfortable with the sidebar and Settings

Spend a few minutes with the sidebar and info pane before you build anything serious. That is where you see which bots exist and switch between them. In Settings, look specifically for the difference between Update and Reset for a bot: one changes how a bot behaves going forward, the other clears it back to a blank state. Confusing the two is a common early mistake, since a "reset" can remove context you assumed was saved.

A simple first routine for multiple bots

You do not need a dozen bots to get value out of Grok Bot. A workable starting layout is one generalist bot for everyday questions, plus a couple of specialists for recurring jobs, like a travel bot or a deals bot. Add a new specialist only when a job keeps coming back and keeps cluttering your generalist's context. The guide's chapter on using more than one bot covers naming, scoping, and handoffs between bots in more depth.

Common first-week mistakes

  • Assuming every plan includes Grok Bot, then troubleshooting a feature that was never available on Free or Plus.
  • Running one bot for everything and losing track of what context it already has.
  • Never opening Settings, so Update and Reset stay a mystery until something unexpected happens.
  • Not checking the shared weekly usage pool, then being surprised by a limit mid-task.
